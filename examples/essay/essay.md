# On Revision

Most writers think revision means fixing sentences. It does, eventually. But the first pass of a real revision is structural, and it asks a harder question: does this piece know what it is about?

A draft that does not know its subject will wander. It will circle back to the same ideas, repeat the same phrases, and pad its transitions. None of that is visible at the sentence level. It shows up in the counts.

That is the case for measuring prose. Numbers cannot tell you whether a paragraph is good. They can tell you where to look.
